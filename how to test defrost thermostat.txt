If all individual physical components pass testing (heater, thermal fuse, NTC sensor, and thermostat/bimetal), but the **Main Control PCB LED flashes 5 times**, the system is experiencing a logical or signal-path fault.

### Understanding the 5-Blink Fault Code

On Samsung Main PCB units, **5 LED blinks** indicates a **Defrost Cycle Timeout / Execution Error**.

The PCB tracks defrost cycles by time and temperature:

* When defrost starts, the PCB expects the NTC sensor resistance to drop as the coil heats up.
* If the maximum defrost run time (typically **60 to 80 minutes**) elapses without the NTC reaching the termination threshold (~10°C / 50°F), the board cuts power, assumes a defrost failure, and flashes **5 blinks**.

---

### Why the Heater Isn't Turning On (4 Direct Causes)

Even though your heater, fuse, and sensors are electrically sound individually, the current path or drive signal is blocked:

```
[Microcontroller] ---> [Transistor/Optocoupler] ---> [Defrost Relay Coil] ---> [Closed Contacts] ---> [Heater]

```

#### 1. Failed / Cold-Soldered Defrost Relay on the Main PCB

The line voltage ($220\text{V}$ / $110\text{V}$) must cross a mechanical relay mounted on the Main Control PCB.

* **The Failure:** The relay coil can burn out, or the mechanical contact points inside the relay carbonize and fail to connect even when the board "clicks."
* **Result:** The microcontroller commands a defrost cycle, but no voltage ever leaves the PCB terminal board down to the freezer harness.

#### 2. Open Circuit in the Wiring Harness or Connectors

Individual components may test fine at their localized plugs, but the signal path to the back panel is broken.

* **The Failure:** A pushed-out pin, corrosion, or a broken wire inside the foam cabinet wall between the Main PCB connector and the freezer back-wall terminal block.
* **Verification:** Measure voltage directly at the heater plug inside the freezer while forcing a defrost cycle.

#### 3. Board-Level Sensor Signal Drift (A/D Converter Input)

While your multimeter might read the NTC sensor as correct at room temperature, the signal may be corrupted before reaching the board's microcontroller.

* **The Failure:** A degraded filter capacitor or pull-up resistor on the NTC sensing line of the Main PCB can cause the microcontroller to perceive the coil as already warm (e.g., falsely reading $20^\circ\text{C}$).
* **Result:** The PCB immediately cancels the defrost command before sending power to the relay because it mistakenly believes no ice is present.

#### 4. System "Heater Time-Out" Lockout

Once the Main PCB logs a 5-blink error state, it often **disables the defrost output relay** on subsequent attempts to prevent potential fire hazards until the power logic is completely reset or forced into test mode.

---

### Field Verification Sequence

1. **Test Defrost Voltage Output at PCB:** Locate the defrost output pin on the Main PCB (refer to the schematic printed on the rear cover). Trigger a forced defrost (or power cycle) and measure AC voltage directly across the **Defrost Relay Output Pin** and **Neutral**.
* *If $0\text{V}$ AC:* The Main PCB relay or relay driver transistor is faulty. The Main PCB must be replaced or repaired.
* *If Line Voltage ($220\text{V}$ / $110\text{V}$) is present at the PCB, but $0\text{V}$ reaches the heater:* There is an open wire/connector in the internal cabinet harness.


2. **Inspect the Defrost Relay Board Traces:** Flip the Main PCB over and check the solder joints under the main black/blue defrost relay for dark rings or charred traces indicating a burnt trace.
